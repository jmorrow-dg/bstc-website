# Money and banking in Bali for foreigners: cards, Wise, QRIS and local accounts (2026)

Most foreigners in Bali don't need an Indonesian bank account: a multi-currency card, a backup card and rupiah from bank-owned ATMs cover daily life. You generally can't open a bank account in Bali as a foreigner until you hold a KITAS or KITAP, because that's what BCA, Mandiri and CIMB Niaga ask for on their own pages (checked 26 October 2026). QRIS, the national QR code, works for visitors only if their home app is linked to it, which covers six countries so far.

This guide is for anyone running a business from Bali: SaaS and agency founders, ecom operators, freelancers and creators. It isn't financial, tax or legal advice. For the bigger picture, see our [first-week checklist](/blog/new-to-bali-first-week-checklist), the [Bali tax guide](/blog/bali-founder-tax-guide-2026) and the [PT PMA playbook](/blog/pt-pma-indonesia-founder-playbook-2026).

## The short answer by situation

| Your situation | What works |
|---|---|
| First trip, visit visa | Multi-currency card, backup card, cash from bank ATMs |
| From Malaysia, Thailand, Singapore, Japan, South Korea or China | All of the above, plus QRIS through your home app |
| Staying a year on an E33G | As above; a local account may be possible once you have your stay permit card |
| KITAS or KITAP holder | Local rupiah account at BCA, Mandiri or CIMB Niaga |
| Hiring locally or invoicing Indonesian clients | PT PMA with a corporate account |

## Paying day to day: cards, cash and ATMs

**Pay in rupiah.** Bali's Governor's Circular 7 of 2025 lists transacting in rupiah and paying with the national QR standard among foreign tourists' obligations ([Bali government](https://bmc.baliprov.go.id/news/title/tertibkan-turis-asing-yang-berulah-di-bali-gubernur-koster-keluarkan-se-nomor-7-tahun-2025), 24 Mar 2025, checked 26 Oct 2026).

**Cards** work at larger places. Wise's Indonesia guide says Visa, Mastercard and American Express are widely accepted at hotels, restaurants and department stores in cities and tourist areas, but you'll need cash for smaller businesses and markets ([Wise Australia](https://wise.com/au/blog/money-and-banks-in-indonesia), checked 26 Oct 2026). When a card machine or ATM offers to charge you in your home currency, say no. That's dynamic currency conversion, and the rate is set by the merchant's bank, not yours.

**ATMs** are easy to find in the busy areas. Things to know, from [Wise's guide to ATMs in Indonesia](https://wise.com/gb/blog/atms-in-indonesia) (checked 26 Oct 2026):

- Indonesian ATMs typically allow **IDR 1.25 million to 3 million per withdrawal**, depending on the machine and your bank.
- The ATM's owner may charge a fee, shown on screen before you confirm. Bank-owned ATMs are the place to start.
- Machines generally take 6-digit PINs but should accept 4-digit ones.
- Your home bank may charge its own overseas fee too.

Because of the per-withdrawal cap, a fixed per-withdrawal fee from your home bank adds up fast. Check your card's foreign ATM fee before you fly.

## QRIS: what it is and whether visitors can use it

**QRIS** (Quick Response Code Indonesian Standard) is the national payment QR code standard set by [Bank Indonesia](https://www.bi.go.id/en/fungsi-utama/sistem-pembayaran/ritel/kanal-layanan/qris/default.aspx). One code at the counter works with any connected bank or wallet app. Bank Indonesia says 96.7% of QRIS merchants are small and micro businesses, and the Love Bali tourist levy accepts it too.

**Visitors can pay with QRIS if their home app is linked.** Bank Indonesia's [cross-border QRIS page](https://www.bi.go.id/id/fungsi-utama/sistem-pembayaran/ritel/kanal-layanan/qris/qris-antarnegara/default.aspx) and a [Bank Indonesia article dated 26 Sep 2026](https://www.bi.go.id/id/publikasi/ruang-media/cerita-bi/Pages/jelajahi-indonesia-makin-mudah-dengan-qris-antarnegara-dan-indonesia-travel-pack.aspx) (both checked 26 Oct 2026) say:

- As of August 2026, cross-border QRIS links **six countries**: Malaysia, Thailand, Singapore, Japan, South Korea and China. Timor-Leste and Hong Kong are in development.
- Those corridors cover about **39.77% of foreign arrivals**, so roughly 60%, including Australians, can't use it yet.
- Inbound payments by foreign visitors reached 17.8 million transactions worth IDR 6.2 trillion by August 2026.

Bank Indonesia lists the home-country apps and banks that work for visitors paying in Indonesia, for example:

| Home country | Network | Examples Bank Indonesia lists |
|---|---|---|
| Malaysia | DuitNow QR | Maybank, CIMB, Public Bank, Touch 'n Go eWallet, Boost |
| Thailand | PromptPay | Bangkok Bank, Kasikornbank, Krungthai, Siam Commercial Bank |
| Singapore | NETS QR | DBS PayLah, OCBC, UOB |
| Japan | JPQR Global | Check with your provider |
| South Korea | Korean QR link | Seoul Pay, Jeju Pay, Kookmin Bank, Woori Card |
| China | UnionPay and Alipay | Alipay, the UnionPay app and major Chinese banks |

To pay, open your app, scan the merchant's QRIS code, enter the amount in rupiah, check the amount in your own currency and confirm with your PIN. Bank Indonesia says merchants pay no extra fee to accept these payments.

**What about everyone else?** In 2026 Bank Indonesia launched the Indonesia Travel Pack, a digital gateway with payment and SIM information that it calls a stopgap for visitors from countries not yet linked. Its first phase covers Jakarta and Banten, not Bali (same article). Indonesian e-wallets such as GoPay, DANA and ShopeePay take QRIS too, but we couldn't confirm on 26 Oct 2026 what each asks a foreigner for to sign up, so we've left that out.

## Wise and other multi-currency cards

A multi-currency card is the simplest setup for most newcomers. Wise, for example, says its card converts at the mid-market rate with a small conversion fee when you pay or withdraw in rupiah ([Wise](https://wise.com/gb/blog/atms-in-indonesia)). Two things to check:

- **The free ATM allowance depends on where your account is.** On 26 Oct 2026, Wise Australia's [card fees page](https://wise.com/au/pricing/card-fees) said withdrawals are free up to AUD 400 a month, then 2.69% of the amount over that. Other countries have different limits.
- **The ATM owner's fee still applies**, whatever card you use.

Carry a second card on a different network (for example, one Visa and one Mastercard) from a different provider, in case one is blocked or swallowed by a machine. Tell your banks you're travelling.

## Can a foreigner open a bank account in Indonesia?

Yes, but usually only once you hold a stay permit. Here's what each bank's own page asked foreigners for on 26 Oct 2026:

| Bank and product | What foreigners need | Source |
|---|---|---|
| BCA Tahapan (rupiah savings) | Passport, plus KITAS or KITAP if you live in Indonesia; foreigners not living in Indonesia may be asked for "other documents" under anti-money-laundering rules. IDR 500,000 opening deposit | [BCA](https://www.bca.co.id/en/Individu/produk/simpanan/Tahapan) |
| Mandiri Tabungan Rupiah | Passport plus KITAS, KITAP or Golden Visa. IDR 500,000 opening deposit | [Mandiri](https://www.bankmandiri.co.id/en/tabungan-rupiah) |
| Mandiri Livin' app, passport sign-up | Foreigners living in Indonesia with a passport (6+ months left) and ITAS, ITAP or Golden Visa | [Mandiri Livin' WNA](https://www.bankmandiri.co.id/en/livin-wna) |
| CIMB Niaga XTRA Savers | Passport, KITAS or KITAP, a copy of your NPWP (tax number), FATCA and CRS declaration | [CIMB Niaga product sheet, June 2026](https://www.cimbniaga.co.id/content/dam/cimb/tabungan/riplay/umum/RIPLAY-UMUM-XTRA-Savers-202606.pdf) |
| Jenius | Indonesian citizens with a KTP only | [Jenius registration FAQ](https://www.jenius.com/en/faq/mengenal-jenius/registrasi) |

What that means in practice:

- **On a visit visa,** plan on not having a local account. BCA's wording leaves a door open for foreigners who don't live in Indonesia, but we couldn't confirm what "other documents" a branch would accept, so ask at a branch before you count on it.
- **On an E33G,** the eVisa site says the remote worker visa comes with a limited stay permit (ITAS), and that a physical card is available from the immigration office ([official eVisa page](https://evisa.imigrasi.go.id/front/faq/e076131c-0d39-469b-afaf-75fc66aff923), checked 26 Oct 2026). That card is the kind of document the banks ask for, but we couldn't confirm on 26 Oct 2026 that each bank accepts E33G holders. Ask the branch.
- **Some products also want an NPWP,** Indonesia's tax number. Getting one ties into your tax position, so read the [tax guide](/blog/bali-founder-tax-guide-2026) first.
- **Bring originals to the branch.** Mandiri's Livin' page lists passport sign-up steps for foreigners, but also showed a "Seamless Registration Available soon" banner when we checked, so the app route may not be open to you yet.

## Sending money in and out

- **Into Indonesia:** if you don't have a local account, you mostly don't need to send money in at all; you pay with your card and withdraw cash. Once you have one, compare the total cost of a transfer, exchange rate included, on each provider's own quote screen before you send.
- **Out of Indonesia:** Indonesian banks can ask for documents showing what a large foreign-exchange transaction is for (Bank Indonesia calls these underlying documents). The thresholds are set in Bank Indonesia regulations and have changed over time, and we couldn't open the current regulation text on 26 Oct 2026, so ask your bank before you move a large amount.
- **Rent and deposits:** landlords and villa managers set their own terms. Get a receipt for every payment.

## Getting paid by overseas clients and customers while in Bali

Start with what your visa allows. The visa on arrival, the C1 visit visa and the E33G remote worker visa pages all say holders can't take pay from people or companies in Indonesia ([B1](https://www.imigrasi.go.id/wna/daftar-visa-indonesia/B1), [C1](https://www.imigrasi.go.id/wna/daftar-visa-indonesia/C1), [E33G](https://evisa.imigrasi.go.id/front/faq/e076131c-0d39-469b-afaf-75fc66aff923), checked 26 Oct 2026). So the usual setup is an overseas company or sole-trader structure, paid into an overseas business account, with clients outside Indonesia. ## Scams and staying safe with money

From the [UK government's Indonesia safety advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/indonesia/safety-and-security) (updated 18 Sep 2026) and Bali's 2025 circular, both checked 26 Oct 2026:

- **Card fraud is common.** Don't let your card out of sight when paying.
- **Fake help numbers on ATMs.** Criminals sometimes stick a fake phone number on an ATM, then ask for your card details when you call. Use your bank's number from its app or the back of your card.
- **Money changers.** Unlicensed changers may short-change you. Licensed ones show a Bank Indonesia logo with a permit number and a QR code; the UK advice says to open the code and check the business name and licence.
- **Bag snatching.** The UK advice says there are often reports of bag snatching in Bali. Carry one card and the cash you need, and leave the backup card somewhere else.

Practical habits that help: use ATMs inside banks or malls in daylight, cover the keypad, count cash at the counter before you leave, and turn on transaction alerts in your banking apps.
